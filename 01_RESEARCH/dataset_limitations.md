# DATASET LIMITATIONS

## 1. Introduction

Although road traffic accident datasets provide valuable information for research and analysis, they may contain limitations that affect the accuracy, completeness, and interpretation of the results.

This document identifies potential limitations associated with the datasets used in the Road Traffic Accident Analysis project.

## 2. Geographical Limitations

The UK Department for Transport Road Safety Open Data represents Great Britain.

It does not directly represent road traffic accident conditions in Rwanda, Kenya, or Uganda.

Therefore, British accident records must not be interpreted as African accident statistics.

Additional African datasets will be investigated separately.

## 3. Data Availability

Not all countries provide publicly accessible accident records.

Some government institutions publish only annual reports or summarized statistics instead of detailed CSV files.

This may limit the ability to perform detailed comparisons between countries.

## 4. Missing Values

Some records may contain missing or unknown information, such as:

- Accident location
- Weather conditions
- Vehicle characteristics
- Driver information
- Casualty age
- Accident time

Missing values may affect statistical calculations and analytical results.

## 5. Data Accuracy

The accuracy of accident records depends on the original reporting and data collection procedures.

Some accidents may not be reported or may be incorrectly recorded.

Consequently, the datasets may not represent every road traffic accident that occurred.

## 6. Different Data Formats

Different countries and organizations may use different:

- Column names
- Date formats
- Measurement units
- Accident classification methods
- Severity categories
- Identification systems

These differences may make combining datasets difficult.

## 7. Reporting Period Limitations

Datasets may cover different years and reporting periods.

Comparisons between countries must consider differences in the periods covered.

## 8. Data Privacy

Some accident records may contain personal or sensitive information.

The project will avoid unnecessary personal information and use only data permitted for academic research.

## 9. Statistical Limitations

The results depend on the completeness, quality, and coverage of the available datasets.

The analysis will identify these limitations when presenting findings and drawing conclusions.

## 10. Mitigation Measures

To reduce the effects of these limitations, the project will:

1. Prioritize official and reliable data sources.
2. Inspect datasets before database implementation.
3. Identify and document missing values.
4. Standardize data formats where appropriate.
5. Avoid combining incompatible datasets.
6. Preserve original downloaded files.
7. Document the geographical coverage of each dataset.
8. Clearly explain limitations when interpreting results.

## 11. Conclusion

Understanding dataset limitations is essential for producing reliable research findings. All analytical results will be interpreted according to the quality, completeness, geographical coverage, and reporting periods of the available data.
